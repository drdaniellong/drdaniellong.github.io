About

Background

I hold a Ph.D. in Sociology from the University of Wisconsin–Madison, with graduate training in demography and economics, and I've spent 30 years analyzing large-scale administrative, longitudinal, and survey data — as a Senior Research Scientist at NWEA/HMH, a Research Scientist at UConn's Neag School of Education, Director of Research at Connecticut Voices for Children, Director of Quantitative Research at Research for Action, and an Assistant Professor of Sociology at Wesleyan University. Across those roles I've built the exact muscle institutional research demands: querying and reconciling administrative and student-level data systems, producing federal and state reporting, and translating statistical findings for non-technical stakeholders — school boards, legislators, journalists, and now, university leadership.

Featured Work

Three projects that show how I work

Selected to cover the full range of the job: modeling student outcomes, working fluently in federal reporting systems, and communicating findings to non-technical decision-makers.

Reporting Pipeline

Simulated Banner-to-IPEDS Reporting Pipeline

An end-to-end simulated reporting workflow built to mirror how institutional research offices actually work: student and institutional data generated using Banner-standard naming conventions, queried and reconciled with Oracle SQL/PL scripts, and compiled into federally uploadable IPEDS survey files using sqlcl and the R package IPEDSuploadables. Demonstrates the full chain from raw administrative tables to submission-ready federal reporting.

Oracle SQL / PL Banner data model sqlcl R / IPEDSuploadables

Retention

Enrollment & Retention Analytics Dashboard

A cohort-tracking dashboard applying the multilevel modeling, growth curve modeling, and propensity-score methods I've used throughout my research career to model first-to-second-year retention risk, built on public IPEDS/College Scorecard data and designed for self-service use by deans and advisors.
